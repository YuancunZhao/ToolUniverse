# ACGS 2024 Benign-Context Summary

Source:

- ACGS Best Practice Guidelines for Variant Classification in Rare Disease 2024, v1.2.

## BA1/BS1

- Generic BA1 5% is usually too high for rare diseases; disease-specific thresholds are preferred.
- BS1 should use disease prevalence, penetrance, inheritance, genetic heterogeneity, and allelic heterogeneity.
- Maximum ancestry AF should be used.
- High-penetrance autosomal dominant disorders may allow `BS1_Strong` as stand-alone likely benign evidence when the threshold is exceeded.
- Check gnomAD version, quality flags, paralogs, and low-coverage regions.

## BS2

- Requires healthy observations that are incompatible with disease penetrance and age of onset.
- For highly penetrant dominant disease, at least two appropriately phenotyped healthy heterozygotes may support BS2.
- For severe pediatric-onset recessive disease, healthy homozygotes or incompatible homozygotes in population data can be useful.
- Late-onset or reduced-penetrance disorders require more healthy individuals and detailed phenotyping.

## BP2

- Applies when another pathogenic variant explains a fully penetrant dominant disorder, or when the variant is in cis with a pathogenic variant.
- Requires phase and phenotype context.
- Do not apply when biallelic disease with a different phenotype has not been excluded.

## BP5

- Applies only when another molecular diagnosis explains the patient's disease.
- Use caution with blended phenotypes and genes where pathogenic variants can co-occur with little phenotype impact.
